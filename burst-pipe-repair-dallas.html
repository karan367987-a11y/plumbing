WARNING SIGNS

How to Know If You Have a Burst Pipe

Recognizing the early signs of a burst pipe can save you thousands in water damage restoration.

Water Stains on Walls or Ceilings

Yellowish or brown discoloration on walls, ceilings, or floors often indicates a hidden leak or burst pipe behind the surface.

Sudden Drop in Water Pressure

If your faucets or shower heads suddenly lose pressure, a burst pipe may be diverting water flow away from your fixtures.

Running Water Sounds

Hearing water running when all taps are off? This could indicate a burst pipe behind walls or under your foundation.

Unexpectedly High Water Bills

A sudden spike in your water bill without increased usage is a classic sign of a hidden leak or burst pipe.

Wet Spots or Puddles

Puddles forming in your yard or wet spots in your basement or crawlspace often indicate a broken underground pipe.

Frozen Pipes

If you have little or no water flow during freezing weather, your pipes may be frozen and at risk of bursting at any moment.

CAUSES

Why Burst Pipes Happen in Dallas

Understanding the common causes of burst pipes helps you take preventive action.

Freezing Temperatures

When water freezes, it expands by about 9%. This pressure can easily burst pipes, especially in unheated areas like garages, attics, and crawlspaces.

Tree Root Intrusion

Tree roots naturally seek water and can penetrate underground pipes, causing cracks and eventual bursts.

Aging & Corrosion

Older pipes, especially galvanized steel, can corrode from the inside over time, weakening walls and leading to bursts.

High Water Pressure

Excessive water pressure puts stress on pipes and fittings, increasing the risk of sudden bursts.

Shifting Soil

Dallas's clay soil expands and contracts with moisture changes, which can shift underground pipes and cause breaks.

Poor Installation

Improperly installed pipes, incorrect fittings, or substandard materials can lead to premature failures and bursts.

PREVENTION

How to Prevent Burst Pipes in Dallas

Follow these expert tips to protect your home from costly pipe bursts this winter and year-round.

Insulate Exposed Pipes

Wrap pipes in unheated areas — including attics, garages, basements, and crawlspaces — with pipe insulation or heat tape.

Let Faucets Drip

During freezing weather, allow a small trickle of water to flow through your faucets. This relieves pressure in the pipes and prevents freezing.

Keep Garage Doors Closed

In cold weather, keep garage doors closed to protect water pipes that run through or near the garage.

Schedule Annual Inspections

Regular plumbing inspections can identify weak spots, corrosion, and minor leaks before they become major bursts.

Regulate Water Pressure

Install a pressure regulator to keep your home's water pressure at safe levels (typically 40-60 PSI).

Drain Outdoor Faucets

Disconnect garden hoses and drain outdoor faucets before winter to prevent freezing and bursting.
